Saturating loops: stability certificates for modulated filters with saturation in the feedback

Can a compiler prove that a filter with tanh, clipping or soft saturation in its feedback never blows up or sticks, however its knobs move? The pass finds the saturators in Field's compiled code and certifies the loop with sector and Lur'e-Lyapunov energy functions. It never certified any of 169 kernels that can blow up or stick, while linearising the saturators certified 7 that can blow up or stick. Coverage held on generated kernels (74.0%) but fell to 56.6% on the blind set, and the tanh Moog reaches Hélie's 5/3 only for cutoff ceilings up to g = 0.5237.

H1–H3 confirmed H4 failed (slew limit adds 0% as registered) 0 false certificates in 416 kernels

Proven-stable modulation for Field filters

Can a compiler prove, from a kernel's code alone, that a filter cannot blow up however fast its knobs move? A quadratic-energy certifier (LMI plus a checked cover) never certified any of 174 kernels an instability oracle can blow up, and certified 92.5% of the generated and 88.7% of the blind stable-looking kernels. Today's frozen-knob check calls 58 of 80 unstable kernels safe. The Moog ladder bound is pinned to k ∈ [2.8848, 2.8856]. A slew limit adds nothing once the cutoff can reach zero.

H1 confirmed within scope H2 killed (15.8%–17.4% vs 25% bar) 272 + 97 held-out kernels

Aliasing-aware compilation for Field

Can a compiler predict how much nonlinear audio code aliases and pick the cheapest oversampling factor per subgraph? Steady-state harmonic prediction confirms H1 (Spearman ρ = 0.94–1.00 across 272 held-out and 97 delay kernels). Per-subgraph oversampling (H2) is killed—with live A/B audio renders showing why real half-band resamplers leak energy across staged cuts. On real music (H5, 1764 kernel-clip pairs against an exact oracle) steady-tone prediction fails: no tested predictor is both safe and cheap.
