# Feel map: how every musical cue maps to feeling (research, 2026-10-28)

Purpose: the table C5 (mood and emotion) needs, on our 22 axes. It replaces the 10-row draft. Every row says where the claim comes from.
Evidence: **A** = measured in a study found this session; **B** = standard finding in the literature, named but not re-opened this session (verify before it becomes a rule); **C** = my inference, never tested.

## Coordinates (decision: three, not two)
Eerola, Ferrer and Alluri 2012 (Music Perception 30:49) split arousal into **energy arousal** and **tension arousal**; they behave differently on timbre.
So every row is scored on: valence (V), energy arousal (E), tension arousal (T). Named emotions (happy, sad, angry, fear, tender, calm) are regions of these three.

## Findings that shape the rule (all A)
| Finding | Source | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Cues mostly act in a straight line and add up (77-89% of rating variance linear, interactions near zero) | Eerola, Friberg, Bresin 2013 (Front. Psychol. 4:487) | a linear rule on the axes is the right first model; test interactions after |
| Cue order of importance for happy/sad/peaceful/scary: mode, tempo, register, dynamics, articulation, timbre | same | weights: mode and tempo largest |
| No single cue means one emotion; any cue can signal several | Gabrielsson and Lindstrom review | never label from one cue; use the whole profile |
| Tempo can overrule mode for happy vs sad | Gabrielsson review | keep interaction check for mode x tempo |
| 50-57% of valence and energy from a few timbre numbers (high/low frequency energy ratio, attack slope, spectral regularity) | Eerola et al. 2012 | timbre rows below |
| Medium syncopation gives most urge to move and pleasure (inverted U) | Witek et al. 2014 PLOS ONE (later 2024 study disputes the complexity effect) | groove rule is a bump, not a line; marked uncertain |

## What is still missing (honest list)
1. Chord types beyond major/minor, progressions, swing and micro-timing: no source found; we measure them on our own data (EMOPIA, 203k mood rows, 965 mood-named chord files), reference only, never truth.
2. Cultural limits: sources are mostly Western listeners.
3. All sources give direction; weights come from fitting on our data and the owner's listening.

## How it is used
| Step | What |
